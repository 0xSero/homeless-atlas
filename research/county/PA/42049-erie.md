## Summary

On the night of January 22, 2026, Erie County's Continuum of Care counted 433 people experiencing homelessness, 394 of them sheltered and 39 unsheltered [1]. That is down from 457 in 2025 and 454 in 2024, but the county's own trend table shows the total was 300 in 2022 and 261 in 2021 [1]. The unsheltered count rose from 0 in 2021 to 76 in 2024 before falling to 47 in 2025 and 39 in 2026 [1][2]. About 600 more people were living in permanent supportive housing or rapid rehousing on count night, a group the report calls "the near homeless" and does not include in the 433 [1]. County overdose deaths fell from 116 in 2023 to 66 in 2024 [6].

## The count

The Department of Human Services says the CoC counts every year in the last week of January, with the sheltered figure taken from the Homeless Management Information System by county staff working with provider agencies [3]. The Erie Times-News reported that the Mercyhurst Civic Institute coordinates the count and that outreach workers and volunteers canvass the streets on count night [5]. The 2025 report credits part of that year's high unsheltered figure to the end of rental assistance funds and to an improved count method with more volunteers [2]. In 2026 the count found 318 people in emergency shelter, 76 in transitional housing or safe havens and 39 unsheltered [1].

The Erie Times-News figure of 1,059 people for January 2023 is a different measure from the HUD point-in-time count, because it adds 688 people already living in permanent supportive housing or rapid rehousing to the 266 in emergency shelter, 56 in transitional housing, 9 in safe havens and 40 unsheltered [5]. Those last four groups total 371, the county's own 2023 point-in-time figure, and people in permanent housing programs count as housed under HUD's definition, so 1,059 should not be compared with the 433 counted in 2026 [1][5].

## Who is homeless here

In 2026 the count found 35 families with children in shelter, 104 people in those families and 65 children under 18 [1]. Adults without children made up 324 sheltered households with 329 people, 26 of them 65 or older [1]. Of all 433 people, 100 (23%) met HUD's definition of chronic homelessness, compared with 112 (25%) in 2025 [1][2]. Twenty households led by veterans, 27 people in all, were in shelter, and 5 of the 39 unsheltered people were veterans [1]. All 39 unsheltered people were adults, and the report says no unsheltered youth were found [1].

## Mental health and addiction

Of the adults counted in 2026, 183 reported mental illness and 140 (43%) reported a substance use disorder [1]. The Erie County Department of Health reports 116 overdose deaths in the county in 2023 and 66 in 2024 [6]. Its Overdose Fatality Review examined 10 deaths in 2025; in 3 of those cases the person was unhoused at the time of death and in 8 the person had been unhoused at some point [6]. That review covers a small, selected set of cases, so it does not measure how many people who died of overdose were homeless [6]. The county's mental health crisis line is 814-456-2014 or 1-800-300-9558 [19].

## Housing and money

The county's 2026 proposed budget lists a state Homeless Assistance Program allocation of $770,791 for fiscal 2024-25, which pays for bridge housing, rent assistance and emergency shelter [7]. The same budget notes that state and HUD grants serve 2,751 homeless or near homeless people in the county, a service count that is broader than the point-in-time count [7]. The City of Erie had $273,428 in Emergency Solutions Grant funds available in its July 2023 to June 2024 program year, and its ESG-funded shelters and transitional housing served 2,262 people [8]. The Housing Authority of the City of Erie said in 2022 that it housed 2,064 families in public housing and nearly 1,100 more with vouchers, and that the wait for two or three bedrooms could be a year [10]. Applicants for public housing choose which site-based waitlists to join [11]. The Legal Services Corporation's tracker counted 16,450 eviction filings in Erie County from March 16, 2020 through its September 2026 update [12].

## Shelters and providers

All emergency shelter in the county is reached through the coordinated entry line, 814-SHELTER (814-743-5837) [4]. Erie City Mission's Samaritan Care shelter can house up to 56 men a night [13]. Erie United Methodist Alliance runs The Refuge, a 32-bed shelter for families with children, and Our Neighbors' Place, a seasonal shelter with a nightly maximum of 50 for the 2026-27 season [14][15]. Community of Caring shelters single men and women [16], and SafeNet runs emergency shelter and transitional housing for survivors of domestic violence [17]. ESG-funded emergency shelters reported 82,845 bed-nights used out of 104,950 available in the city's 2023 program year, a utilization rate of 78.94% [8]. The county resource guide also lists Community Shelter Services and Safe Journey among shelter providers [18].

## Local government and law

The Erie County Department of Human Services is the CoC's collaborative applicant and HMIS lead, contracts with and monitors provider agencies, and through its Offices of Mental Health/Intellectual Disabilities serves as the Home Team's lead applicant agency [4][25]. The City of Erie's Department of Economic and Community Development administers the city's Emergency Solutions Grant [9]. In July 2024 then County Executive Brenton Davis described a plan to replace overflow shelters with a Community Resilience Center in the former county work release center [22]. Police cleared a camp of more than 30 people behind the Erie Tool building in 2024 before volunteers removed it [23]. Neither report cites a city or county ordinance on camping, and we found none posted by the city or county [22][23]. Records requests go to the County Solicitor as Open Records Officer, to the City of Erie's Right-to-Know officers, and to the Housing Authority's designated Open Records Officer [26][27][28].

## What is changing

Housing First Erie, announced in 2024 by the Erie Community Foundation and partners, plans a new 50-unit building and the renovation of 100 existing units to house 150 people who are chronically homeless [20][21]. The Erie Reader reported about $15 million pledged toward the project in June 2024 [20]. Our Neighbors' Place now operates at the Community Resilience Center at 450 East 16th Street, open November 29, 2026 to March 31, 2027 [15]. Erie City Mission reported in October 2025 that its outreach teams had visited nearly 400 encampments in the past year [24]. The 2026 count report notes that multiple new encampments were identified as street outreach expanded [1].

## Gaps in the record

No public source we found lists an emergency shelter that takes unaccompanied minors or young adults without a court or child welfare referral; the Department of Human Services could publish one [18]. The Housing Authority's application page gives no current waiting list counts or wait times, so the 2022 figures above are the latest we found [10][11]. The CoC posts point-in-time snapshots but no yearly HMIS count of everyone served, which the HMIS lead could publish [3]. Eviction figures here are a national tracker's running total, not a yearly count published by the county courts [12]. The overdose review covers 10 cases and does not report how many of the 66 deaths in 2024 were among homeless people [6]. Records contacts here come from each agency's own posting, not the state Office of Open Records directory [26][27][28].
