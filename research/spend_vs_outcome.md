# spend-vs-outcome — federal money next to CoC outcomes

Two derived metrics that put spend and outcome side by side per Continuum of Care, plus the site table
that shows them. This is a **derivation, not a domain**: the builder is
`scripts/build_outcome_metrics.py` (a metric builder like `scripts/build_turnover.py`), it fetches
nothing of its own, and it can be re-run whenever `award`, `pit`, `geo_coc` or the `spm` metrics change.

| | |
|---|---|
| builder | `scripts/build_outcome_metrics.py` (`--dry-run`, `--report`, `--verbose`), plus the thin wrapper `ingest/domains/spend_vs_outcome.py` so `python3 -m ingest.run spend_vs_outcome` re-derives the same rows, records `ingest_run`, and still needs no network |
| writes | `metric` rows: `entity_type='coc'`, `metric='spend_per_exit_to_housing'` / `'spend_per_person_placed'`, `is_est=1`, formula in `note` |
| private tables | `spend_vs_outcome_place` (35,183 county/place → CoC resolutions), `spend_vs_outcome_spend` (7,889 CoC-years of attributed federal obligation) |
| verifier | `scripts/verify_spend_vs_outcome.py` (live re-check of the five rows below; writes nothing) |
| site | `site/rankings.html` → **"Most and least spent per exit to housing"**, built by `_spend_block()` in `scripts/build_site.py` |
| run | `python3 -m ingest.run spend_vs_outcome` (or `python3 scripts/build_outcome_metrics.py --report`), then `python3 scripts/build_site.py` to republish — the builder makes no request of its own |

## The formulas, exactly

    spend_per_exit_to_housing = federal obligated (place of performance inside the CoC)
                                / spm_exit_to_ph_count            same CoC, same fiscal year

    spend_per_person_placed   = federal obligated (place of performance inside the CoC)
                                / (pit_total(FY) - pit_total(FY+1))   only when that is > 0

**Numerator.** `SUM(award.amount_obligated)` over `award_type` grant/contract (`subaward` rows are
excluded — a subaward is part of its prime's obligation and adding both double counts), attributed to
a CoC only through an award's place of performance. `award.fy` is the federal fiscal year of the
award's **base obligation date** (`period start` where that is absent), and `amount_obligated` is
USASpending's `Total Obligation` for the award, so a FY slice is an award cohort, not that year's
outlays.

**Geography.** County name → county internal point (`geo_city.kind='county'`, Census gazetteer) →
point-in-polygon against HUD's CoC grantee-area layer (`geo_coc.geometry`, ingested by
`coc_geometry`). A named city decides when it resolves (`geo_city.kind='place'` point-in-polygon),
because USASpending's place-of-performance record carries both the city and the county for the same
place and a city polygon is strictly more specific. Overlapping polygons (HUD draws each grantee area
as its own feature, so a Balance-of-State polygon geometrically contains the city CoCs inside it) are
resolved to the **smallest** polygon that contains the point — the most specific planning body.
County names that no longer exist are handled: Connecticut's 2022 planning regions are matched by
suffix ("Capitol Planning Region"), and legacy county names (HARTFORD, FAIRFIELD, …) fall through to
the city. Bare `BALTIMORE`/`ST. LOUIS`/`FAIRFAX` (county and independent city share a name in one
state) resolve by the CITY/COUNTY word in the award's own string, else to the county-shaped body.
Every resolution is stored with its `method` in `spend_vs_outcome_place`, so any single attribution can
be audited with one query.

**Denominator rules.** Zero or missing exits → no row. A PIT count that rose or stayed flat →
`spend_per_person_placed` is **absent**, never negative and never flipped (2,002 CoC-years). A missing
PIT pair in either year → absent (403 CoC-years).

**Provenance.** Every metric row carries `source_id` = the USASpending award source (id 1) for the
money side, and its note names both numbers, both source rows (money 1, SPM workbook 13218, HUD PIT
16) and the arithmetic. Nothing here is a published figure; `is_est=1` on all 5,472 rows.

* **Idempotent**: two consecutive runs → 5,472 metric rows, 7,889 `spend_vs_outcome_spend` rows, 35,183

* **All rows are marked derived**: 3,725 `spend_per_exit_to_housing` + 1,747 `spend_per_person_placed`

* **No impossible values**: `spend_per_exit_to_housing` ≤ $309,623 and ≥ $31 across FY2024's 369

  HOPWA and DOJ money are in this archive and not in the CoC Program award.

## Coverage

* **382 CoCs get `spend_per_exit_to_housing`, 379 get `spend_per_person_placed`** (target ≥200 with both
  inputs). Per year: 363 (2015) → 379 (2024) CoCs for the exit metric, with 3,725 CoC-years in total;
  1,747 CoC-years for the per-person metric.
* **FY2024**: 379 CoC-years have money and exits, 369 of them with ≥50 exits (the site's ranking
  threshold). Federal obligation attributed: $6.52 B.
* **CoC universe**: HUD's `geo_coc` holds 414 CoC numbers, 387 of which have a polygon in the
  grantee-area layer ingested by `coc_geometry`. 383 CoCs receive attributed money; the 31 that do not
  are the 27 polygon-less CoCs listed below plus **AS-500, GU-500, MP-500, VI-500** (no county or place
  centroids exist for the territories at all).

### What is missing, and which way it biases

1. **27 CoCs have no polygon in the HUD layer** (`AR-502, AR-504, AR-506, AR-509, AR-510, AR-512,
   CA-528, MA-501, MA-508, MA-510, MA-514, MA-517, MA-519, MD-500, MD-502, MD-507, MD-508, MD-509,
   MD-510, MD-512, MI-513, NY-504, NY-506, NY-516, NY-607, NY-609, TX-625`). They are historical or
   consolidated CoCs whose ground is now inside a Balance-of-State polygon, so they get **no ratio at
   all**, and their federal dollars are credited to the BoS CoC that now covers the same counties
   (Maryland's rural money lands on MD-514, Michigan's on MI-513's successor MI-500, Arkansas' on
   AR-500, and Holyoke/Hampden/Hampshire's on MA-504, MA-507 and MA-516). **Direction: the BoS CoCs' $/exit is inflated in the years those
   smaller CoCs existed; nobody else's is.** A time-stamped CoC→county crosswalk would fix it; HUD does
   not publish one machine-readably.
2. **13.0 % of obligated dollars ($15.88 B) never reaches a CoC numerator**: $15.57 B of it has no
   place of performance in the federal record at all (USASpending omits it) and $0.25 B names a place
   that resolves to nothing (territories, CT residual). Those awards are in no CoC's numerator.
3. **Territories** (AS, GU, MP, VI) have CoC numbers and SPM measures but no county/place centroids in
   `geo_city`, so their money cannot be attributed and they have no ratio.
4. **The numerator is federal only.** ESG formula money as *spent by states*, city general funds,
   county hospital/justice costs, philanthropy and Medicaid are not in it, and a CoC is a planning
   body, not a spending unit. The ratio therefore cannot separate CoC spend from the city and county
   spend in the same place — the site says so above the table, in the table note, and in both metrics'
   notes.
5. **FY2020 pairs are contaminated**: HUD's January 2021 PIT count was not a normal count (nationally
   54,504 unsheltered people against 226,080 a year earlier), so 2020→2021 "net declines" are mostly
   an artefact. Those rows are kept and flagged in the note (`WARNING: …`) rather than dropped, because
   dropping them would hide a real data-collection event.
6. **Award vintage, not outlay.** Because the numerator is award cohorts keyed on the base obligation
   date, a CoC whose big renewal award was dated in FY2019 and whose exits are counted in FY2024 shows
   a small FY2024 numerator. This is one of the "accounting artefact" mechanisms the site names.

## First look (FY2024, 369 CoCs with ≥50 exits)

| quartile of $ per exit | range | median exit-to-PH rate | median returns per 100 exits | median exits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 cheapest | $31 – $5,754 | 0.443 | 6.77 | 373 |
| Q2 | $5,842 – $12,933 | 0.355 | 5.83 | 438 |
| Q3 | $12,969 – $24,988 | 0.347 | 6.49 | 525 |
| Q4 dearest | $25,093 – $309,623 | 0.318 | 6.98 | 515 |

`corr(log $/exit, exit-to-PH rate) = -0.27` (n=369). Spending more per exit does **not** predict a
better exit rate here; if anything the relation is inverted, and it should not be read causally in
either direction — the exit count is the denominator *and* a component of the rate, small CoCs move
both, and the Q4 CoCs are disproportionately places that host a federal grantee (Howard County, Albany,
Harrisburg, San Francisco) rather than places that spend more on homelessness. The honest claim is the
one on the page: the ranking is a question to ask a CoC, not a verdict.
