About

What this is

A public-record atlas, built from primary sources, of how the United States counts, funds, houses, punishes and treats homeless people.

What is here

Homeless Atlas joins the datasets that normally live apart:

  • 7,412 Point-in-Time count rows — HUD's January street and shelter counts, by Continuum of Care, by year.
  • 18,264 federal awards — every contract, grant and cooperative agreement whose purpose is homelessness, with recipient.
  • 14,095 organizations and 477 named officers — from USASpending, IRS Form 990 filings and SAM registrations.
  • 4,210 institutions — state psychiatric hospitals, psychiatric units, prisons and jails, open and closed, with beds and, where published, budgets.
  • 0 law rows, 0 media items, 0 documents.

What this is not

It is not a list of homeless people. No individual experiencing homelessness is identified anywhere in this dataset. It is not a claim that homeless people are criminals: no national crime dataset records housing status, and any page that puts crime numbers next to homeless counts says so explicitly. It is not legal advice.

Why

Because the money is public and the outcome is visible on the street. If a city spends $100,000 per person housed while the count rises, that is a fact that should be one search away, with a citation — not a press release.

Corrections

Every row carries its source and retrieval date. If a number here disagrees with its source, the source wins — open an issue with the row's source URL.
