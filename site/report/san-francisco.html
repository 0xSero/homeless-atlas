San Francisco

The flagship case study: counts, contracts, organizations, institutions, law and cost.

Generated from the Homeless Atlas database on 2026-11-19. Every number below is followed by the public source it came from; a dagger (†) marks a value this project derived rather than a published figure, and the formula is always stated. Nothing here is hand-typed.

San Francisco is the case study because it is the most expensive and most documented homelessness system in the United States: a city-county of ~800,000 people that spends more per homeless resident than almost any other jurisdiction, inside a region with one of the highest unsheltered counts in the country.

1. How many people, and in what condition

San Francisco County (CoC CA-501) is the case study. In the January 2025 point-in-time count the San Francisco Continuum of Care counted 8,671 people homeless [HUD](https://www.huduser.gov/portal/sites/default/files/xls/2007-2025-PIT-Counts-by-CoC.xlsb). Of those, 4,354 were unsheltered and 4,317 were in shelter. The same count reported that 3,214 people met the federal definition of chronic homelessness and 580 were veterans. Subpopulation columns HUD does not publish for this CoC and year are omitted here rather than estimated.

Between 2007 and 2025 the count moved from 5,703 to 8,671 (+52.0%). A change in this series is not automatically a change on the street: CoCs change count methods, and HUD changes definitions.

10. What it would cost to end it here

  • 8,671 people × $700.00K† (permanent supportive housing, national capital/operating range) = $6.07B†
  • 8,671 people × $60.00K† (rapid rehousing, national capital/operating range) = $520.26M†
  • 8,671 people × $45.00K† (shelter bed, national capital/operating range) = $390.19M†

These are order-of-magnitude figures built from published per-unit costs, not a budget. They exist to make the comparison with annual spending possible: compare with the federal obligated total in section 3 and with the city budget items listed in Documents.
