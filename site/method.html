Method

How every number is produced

Definitions, joins, and the places where the data lies to you.

Definitions

PIT count
HUD's Point-in-Time count: people in shelters and on the street on one night in the last ten days of January. It is a census of visible homelessness, not an estimate of everyone who is homeless during a year.
CoC
Continuum of Care: the HUD-designated regional body that plans and receives homelessness funding. There are ~400. Counts are published by CoC, never by city limit — that is why city numbers here point to their CoC.
Unsheltered
People counted outdoors, in vehicles, or in places not meant for habitation. The least reliable and most policy-sensitive number in the whole system.
Chronically homeless
HUD definition: a disabling condition plus either 12 months continuous homelessness or four episodes in three years.
Federal award value
Obligated amount from USASpending. Obligated ≠ spent, and place of performance ≠ where services happen. Contracts and grants are counted separately and deduplicated by generated award key.
† derived
Anything marked † is computed by us (a rate, a per-person figure, a per-bed cost). It is never presented as published.

Known biases and traps

  • Count methods differ by CoC. Some do a full census, some sample, some use HMIS extrapolation. Year-over-year change in a single CoC can be a method change, not a trend.
  • Federal money is not the whole budget. City general funds, county health dollars, Medicaid and philanthropy are mostly outside USASpending. Where city or state budgets are included they are labelled.
  • Nonprofit revenue is self-reported and lagged: a Form 990 covers a fiscal year that may be 18 months old when filed.
  • Institutional capacity figures come from many different agencies with different definitions of a "bed". Where a state publishes licensed beds we use that; where it publishes staffed beds we say so.
  • Crime. UCR/NIBRS do not record housing status. Area rates are context only. Studies that do measure incidents are listed in Documents, with their own methodology.
  • Substance use and mental illness. PIT subpopulation counts are CoC-reported, self-reported or observed, and systematically undercount. State prevalence estimates (NSDUH) are modelled survey estimates, not counts of homeless people.

Pipeline

Each source has an ingestor in ingest/domains/ that caches the raw response under data/raw/, records the URL, retrieval time, byte size and SHA-256 in the source table, and writes rows that reference it. The site is generated from the database by scripts/build_site.py. Nothing on this site is hand-typed.
